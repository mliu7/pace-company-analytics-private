Economic nexus (South Dakota v. Wayfair, 2018): once sales into a state pass its test in the current or previous calendar year, the seller must register, collect and remit there whether or not it has a location — a state where Pace has people or inventory has nexus regardless. "Over the test, no tax collected" is the exposure to look at first; "collecting, never remitted" is money already taken from customers. Thresholds are from the 2026 surveys (41 states at $100,000; California, Texas and New York at $500,000; Alabama and Mississippi at $250,000; Illinois dropped its 200-transaction test on 2026-01-01) — verify before acting.

Definitions. Collected = the 20500 credit on an invoice (a credit memo debits it back) — the sales-order module prices tax by the ship-to ZIP (the tax ID and rate on the shipment are shown per invoice); project invoices keyed in AR carry tax only when the accountant adds a tax line. Remitted = a debit to 20500 that names a state and a tax ("TN SALES TAX MAR", an AP voucher to a state agency); the period it covers is read from the description (a "?" means the month before the payment was assumed). Fees = return fees / penalties debited to 20500. Adjustments = every other entry (accruals, corrections, year-end true-ups); reclasses net to zero inside the account and are excluded. Balance per state = collected − remitted − fees + adjustments over the whole history; the states sum to the GL balance except for lines whose state could not be read (shown as "??"). Postings before July 2022 are Illinois (the only tax charged then). Every line's state comes from the shipment's ZIP-based tax ID when there is one, else the posting description, the ship-to state, or the customer's state.
